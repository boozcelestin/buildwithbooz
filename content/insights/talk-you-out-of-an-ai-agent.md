I build AI agents. I spent close to a thousand hours this year building them, breaking them, and building them again.

So this may sound strange coming from me. Most service businesses should not buy one yet. And most of the ones that think they need one are not ready.

That is not a sales tactic. It is the most useful thing I can tell you, and almost nobody selling AI will say it, because there is no money in it.

## The pitch this fall

The message to trades owners right now is loud and it is the same everywhere. Let the software run the shop.

This month the biggest software company in the trades told contractors its AI can "orchestrate nearly the entire business." Every week another tool promises to answer, book, follow up, dispatch and invoice on its own.

Some of that will work, for some businesses. But look at what the people who make these systems are doing.

OpenAI paused training of its newest models twice this fall after its own agents got out of the test environment they were supposed to stay inside. Separately, Reuters reported that one hacker used AI agents to break into more than 100 company websites for about $50 each. The technology is powerful. It is also being handed keys before anyone has figured out the locks.

None of that means agents are bad. It means they are a power tool. And a power tool in a shop with no plan just helps you make mistakes faster.

## The real question

The question is never "should I get an AI agent." That is the tool talking.

The real question is where your business is actually leaking, and whether an agent is even the thing that fixes it. Most of the time, it is not.

Bill Gates said it years ago, and it is still the best rule I know. Automation applied to an efficient operation magnifies the efficiency. Automation applied to an inefficient operation magnifies the inefficiency.

## Three signs you are not ready

**The way a job moves through your business lives in one person's head.** If nobody has written down how a call becomes a booked job, how an estimate gets followed up, or how an invoice gets chased, an agent has nothing to follow. It will just automate the chaos, faster.

**Your real bottleneck is somewhere else.** Sometimes the thing choking the business is pricing. Or hiring. Or one person doing the work of three. An agent does nothing for any of that. Buying one just puts something shiny on top of the actual problem.

**You are shopping, not solving.** If you cannot say in one sentence what the agent would do and what it would be worth in dollars, you are buying a tool, not fixing a problem.

## What ready actually looks like

The businesses that get real value from this have four things in common:

1. **One clear process that happens over and over.** Missed call text back. Estimate follow up. Appointment reminders. Something boring and repeatable.
2. **Inputs clean enough to trust.** A real calendar. Real customer records. Not three spreadsheets and a whiteboard.
3. **A real bottleneck a machine can actually remove.** Work that eats hours and needs no judgment.
4. **A person who owns the result.** Someone checks the work, especially early, and steps in when something looks wrong.

That is a much smaller group than the hype suggests. That is fine.

## Why ready usually means built for you

Here is the part the demos hide. When a business genuinely is ready, the thing that works is rarely the off the shelf version.

The value is in the wiring. How it plugs into your phones, your schedule, your way of doing the job. A demo shows you a perfect shop that does not exist. Your shop has a dispatcher who knows which customer hates calls before 9am, and a tech who always runs 20 minutes late on Tuesdays. The system has to fit that, or it fails quietly.

## Where I land

As someone who builds these, here is my honest position. Most of you reading this do not need an AI agent yet. A simpler fix, or no fix at all, will serve you better than the expensive thing everyone is selling.

Start with the leak, not the tool. Find the one place where calls, estimates or jobs are slipping, and fix that first. Sometimes the fix is a system. Sometimes it is a checklist and a person who owns it.
