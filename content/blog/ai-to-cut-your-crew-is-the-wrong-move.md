Companies blamed AI for 112,713 job cuts through July.

If you only read the headlines, the story is simple. AI is here, and it is coming for the crew.

The labor data does not back that up. And the small businesses actually using AI are telling a very different story.

## Where the 112,713 comes from

That number comes from Challenger, Gray & Christmas. It counts what companies say in their layoff announcements. When a company names AI as a reason, the cut goes in the count.

Challenger itself says that is not an independent measure of why the cuts happened. It is what the company said, not what the data shows. One labor economist put it flat: "we are not seeing that in the labor data."

## What small employers actually said

The New York Fed asked more than 5,000 small employers about AI. Three findings stand out.

- **The ones using AI were far more likely to expect to grow headcount.** Net +33 for AI users. Net +15 for everyone else.
- **The gap held even after controlling for firms that were already growing.** So it is not just healthy shops being healthy.
- **77% said AI had not changed their labor costs at all.**

One honest limit. The survey asked what owners expect to do, not what they did. Expectations are not hires. But it points the opposite way from the headline, and it comes from the people closest to the work.

## Both can be true

Big companies can cut and blame AI. Small shops can adopt AI and plan to hire. The difference is what you point it at.

Point AI at your people and you save one salary. You also lose everything that person knew. Which customer hates a call before 9am. Which address has the dog in the yard. Which commercial account always pays late, whatever the invoice says.

No software has that. It lived in a person, and you let the person go.

Point it at the work nobody wants instead. The 9pm callback. The third reminder text. Chasing the invoice from three weeks ago. Your best tech gets his evenings back, and that shop grows.

## In the trades, it is not even close

Plumbing alone faces a shortage of about 550,000 workers by 2027, according to an industry study. Nobody is firing plumbers. Owners are turning work away because they cannot find them.

When you cannot hire, every hour your people spend on the wrong work is a job you could have booked and did not.

## What to point it at first

Make a list of every task your best person does in a week that does not need their license or their judgment. Most shops find the same few:

1. Confirming tomorrow's appointments.
2. Calling back the customer who left a voicemail at 9pm.
3. Sending the second and third reminder on an unpaid invoice.
4. Answering "are you on your way?"

Those are boring, repeatable jobs, and simple automation handles them well. None of them needs an AI agent. Start there, and measure the hours you get back.
