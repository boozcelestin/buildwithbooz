Google's AI may have already called your shop.

Not a customer pretending to be Google. Google itself, through an automated assistant. And what it heard can end up in what Google shows people about your business.

Here is what is happening, why it matters more than it sounds, and what to check this week.

## Two kinds of calls

**Calls for a customer.** Google Search lets people ask Google to call local businesses for them. A customer picks an option like having AI check pricing, Google's assistant calls around, and the answers get sent back to the customer by text or email. Google's help pages say these calls can ask about price and availability, check wait times, and in some cases book appointments.

**Calls to check your profile.** Separately, Google makes its own automated calls and texts to confirm details on your Business Profile, things like hours, parking, or whether you take walk ins. These can create pending updates to your profile.

Availability depends on your state, your category and your language. As of August, the customer calling feature was available for eligible US businesses outside Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana and Nebraska. Check Google's help pages for your area.

## The new Collected Info tab

In September, Google confirmed a new section in Business Profile called Collected Info. It lists details Google gathered from phone or chat conversations with your business, shows how each one was collected, and lets you delete the ones that are wrong. Deleting removes them from Google Search and Maps.

That means a 30 second call your office answered on a busy Tuesday can turn into a public fact about your business.

## Why this matters more than it sounds

Google has been clear that its AI features draw on Business Profile information. That profile is what feeds the AI answers people see when they search, and it is one of the places ChatGPT and other tools look too.

So your profile is no longer just a listing you set up once. It is something AI reads, and now something AI calls.

And it lands on the same weak spot we keep coming back to. Who answers the phone, and what do they say? A missed call already costs you the job. Now a badly handled one can also put the wrong information in front of the next customer.

## What to check this week

1. **Open the Collected Info tab.** It sits inside the edit profile area of your Business Profile in Search or Maps. Read every item. Delete anything wrong.
2. **Brief whoever answers the phone.** An automated call from Google is real, not spam. Decide what they should say when it asks about price, availability or booking, and what they should never promise.
3. **Review your automated call and booking settings.** Business Profile has settings for automated bookings, inquiries and profile updates. Decide what you want on and what you want off. Turning some of it off does not stop every call from Google, so your team still needs a way to handle them.
4. **Finish the basics.** Right primary category, every service you offer, real hours, the towns you cover. The more complete the profile, the less Google has to guess or call to find out.
5. **Watch for suggested edits.** Google now emails owners when someone suggests a change to their Maps listing and gives a few days to respond. Do not let those emails sit.

## The bigger point

Every new search feature this year points the same direction. The AI is moving from listing businesses to talking to them, and soon to booking them.

That makes the boring stuff more important, not less. A complete profile. Accurate details. Someone who answers the phone and knows what to say.
