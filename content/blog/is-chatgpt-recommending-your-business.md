Open ChatGPT and type: "Who is the best plumber in [your town]? My water heater is leaking."

Then look at the list it gives you. Is your shop on it?

That one question is worth more than most marketing advice you will hear this year. A growing share of people now ask an AI before they ask Google. BrightLocal found 45% of consumers use AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity to find local businesses, up from 6% a year earlier.

Here is how to run the test properly, where the AI gets its information, and what to fix if you are not showing up. And one thing almost nobody tells you: showing up is only half the job.

## How to run the test

Do it the way a customer would. A few tips so the results mean something:

- **Use a real problem, not just your trade.** "Best HVAC company in Hialeah" is fine. "My AC is blowing warm air, who can come today in Hialeah" is how people actually ask.
- **Try three or four versions.** A routine job, an emergency, a specific service you want more of, and a neighboring town you serve.
- **Try more than one AI.** ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google's AI Mode can give different answers.
- **Write down what it says about you,** not just whether you appear. Wrong hours, the wrong service area, or an old phone number are all problems worth fixing.

If you show up, good. Check that the details are right. If you do not, the next section is why.

## Where the AI gets its information

Nobody outside these companies knows exactly how they choose who to recommend. What we do know is what they read.

- **Your Google Business Profile.** Google says its AI features draw on Business Profile data, so a complete, accurate profile matters more than ever.
- **Yelp.** In July, Yelp licensed its reviews, ratings, photos and business details to OpenAI for ChatGPT. Even if you have ignored Yelp for years, ChatGPT may not.
- **Your website.** If your site does not plainly say what you do, which towns you serve, and how to reach you, the AI has to guess. It usually guesses someone else.
- **Your reviews.** What people say, how recently they said it, and whether they mention the actual job and the town.

And the direction is clear. This month ServiceTitan launched a way for ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude to book its contractors directly. The AI is moving from recommending to booking.

## Why showing up is not the win

Here is the part the "get found by AI" crowd skips.

People do not take the AI's word for it. Yext found more than 93% of people take at least one step to check before they act on an AI answer. 62% go straight to Google. 58% go to the business's own website.

So the AI puts you on a shortlist. Then the customer checks you out. Your reviews, your profile, your website and whoever answers your phone decide whether you get the job.

Ten years running Endgame Marketing taught me the same lesson about the Google map pack. Ranking is nice. Getting picked is what pays. AI did not change that. It added one more place where you either show up or you don't.

## What to fix this week

1. **Claim and correct your Yelp listing.** Hours, services, service area, photos, phone number. It takes 20 minutes and it now feeds ChatGPT.
2. **Finish your Google Business Profile.** Right primary category, every service you offer, real hours, the towns you cover.
3. **Say it plainly on your website.** Your trade, your towns, your services, and your phone number at the top of the page. Write it the way a customer would ask for it.
4. **Ask every finished job for a review.** Steady new reviews beat a pile of old ones. Ask customers to mention what you fixed.
5. **Answer the phone.** The customer who found you through AI and checked your reviews still calls. If that call rings out, everything above was for nothing.

## The bigger point

Every few years there is a new place to be found. The yellow pages. Google. The map pack. Now AI.

The places change. The job does not. Be easy to find, look trustworthy when they check, and pick up when they call.
