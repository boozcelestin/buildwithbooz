Ask a trades owner how business is and you will hear some version of this.

"We can't find techs."
"I had a good guy. He left for more money."
"We're booked out two weeks and I'm turning work away."

They are not making excuses. And part of where those techs went is a story most owners have not connected yet.

The AI boom.

## Where the techs went

Every big AI company is racing to build data centers. Those buildings run on electricians and HVAC people, and they need a lot of them at once.

Data center jobs pay 25 to 30% more than regular construction work. In parts of Arizona, the wait for HVAC service went from 2 days to 5 or 6.

That does not explain every tech who left. Pay, burnout and a bad boss still do plenty of damage on their own. But it is a new pull, and it is pulling the same licensed people you need.

Your best tech may not have quit the trade. He may be wiring a building full of computers.

## The strange part

The same AI that pulled your guy away is now being sold back to you as the answer.

Some of it can help. But not the way the pitch usually goes. Software is not going to crawl into an attic or pull wire. It is not replacing anyone on your crew.

## You cannot outbid a data center

A big contractor on a data center job can pay what you cannot. Raising your rates to match is not a plan most shops can survive.

So the only move left is to protect the hours of the people you still have.

## Where a tech's day actually goes

Look at what eats a tech's day that is not the job.

- Calling to confirm tomorrow's appointments.
- Driving back to the shop because nobody checked the parts.
- Writing up tickets at the kitchen table at 10pm.
- Calling a customer back about a bill.

None of that needs a license. All of it burns the most expensive hours in your business.

## How to find the hours in one week

You do not need software to start. You need one honest week.

Ask each tech to jot down, at the end of every day, anything they did that was not diagnosing, fixing or installing. Not to the minute. Rough is fine. A note on their phone works.

At the end of the week, add it up. Then sort the list into three piles:

1. **Things a text or reminder could handle on its own.** Appointment confirmations. "On my way" messages. Review requests.
2. **Things the office should own.** Checking parts before the truck leaves. Billing questions. Rescheduling.
3. **Things that really need the tech.** Keep those. That is the job you hired him for.

Fix the first pile first. It is cheap, it is boring, and every hour you pull out of it is an hour back on a paying job.
