HVAC · Hialeah

How a Miami HVAC shop stopped missing 20 calls a week

By Booz · June 26, 2026 · 7 min read

The result$8,450 a month in missed calls, recovered. Response time went from 2 hours 47 minutes to under 30 seconds, with no new hire and no new ads.
[ hero image ]
hvac-shop-hero.jpg

Twenty calls a week. Gone. That is what this shop was losing before anyone noticed, and every one of those calls was a homeowner with a dead AC in a Miami summer, ready to pay, dialing the next guy when nobody picked up.

The owner runs twelve trucks out of a yard in Hialeah. Good crew. Full schedule. Strong reviews. On paper the business looked healthy. Underneath it was bleeding, and the leak had nothing to do with the work. It was the phone.

The problem

Here is how a missed call actually happens. A homeowner's AC dies at 2pm. They search, they find three companies, they call all three. The first one to answer and sound like they know what they are doing wins the job. That is the whole contest. It is over in about four minutes.

This shop was answering in two hours and forty seven minutes on average. By then the homeowner had booked someone else and moved on. The call went to voicemail. The voicemail sat untouched until the next morning. The job was already gone.

Nobody was lazy. The office manager was buried. The techs were on roofs. The phone rang while everyone was already doing something, and the calls piled up in a voicemail box that may as well have been a trash can.

[ inline image ]
hvac-dispatch-board.jpg

The dispatch board before. Every sticky note is a call somebody meant to return.

He had already thrown money at it. A bigger directory listing. A second office line. A note taped to the manager's monitor that said answer the phone. None of it worked, because none of it touched the actual gap, which was the ninety seconds between a call coming in and a human being free to take it.

I counted it over two weeks. Twenty three missed calls in seven days. At their average job value, that was roughly $8,450 walking out the door every month. Not from bad work. From silence.

What I built

A Lead Response System. Simple to describe, and it changed everything: never let a lead sit.

The moment a call comes in and nobody picks up, the system texts the caller back within thirty seconds. Not a robot voice. A real text that says the shop got their call, asks what is going on with their unit, and offers the next two open slots. If they reply, it books them and pings the office. If they go quiet, it follows up twice more over the next day.

The phone stopped being a leak and started being a funnel. Same calls. Completely different outcome.

When a caller picks a slot, the system drops it on the schedule and pings the office manager so she can confirm the tech and the timing. She went from chasing voicemails every morning to glancing at a clean list of customers who already want to book. The work got lighter, not heavier.

I wired it to the line they already had, so the crew did nothing different. No new app. No new habit. No training day. It runs whether the office manager is at her desk or out to lunch.

The result

First thirty days. Response time dropped from two hours and forty seven minutes to under thirty seconds. Missed calls stopped turning into lost jobs and started turning into booked ones.

The shop booked an extra nineteen jobs that month that would have gone to a competitor. The owner did not hire anyone. He did not run a single new ad. The leads were already coming in. They were just falling through the floor, and now the floor was sealed.

He put it plainly on our review call. He thought he had a marketing problem. He had a phone problem.

Three months in, the pattern held. The shop is not answering more calls than before. It is losing almost none of them. That is the whole difference between a business that feels stuck and one that finally keeps what it earns.
